## No-regret and fictitious-play inspired response selection

Consider what has been revealed about the buyer. Strategically reason about their preferences, separating explicit requirements, reasonable inferences and unresolved uncertainty. Treat a nonbinding target differently from a hard budget. In round 2, update your beliefs using only your addressed follow-up and supplied prior state; a buyer's report of a rival offer is reported evidence, not direct access to the rival's catalog or current reply.

Use a no-regret perspective when formulating your response. Compare the purchase revenue you expect from each candidate with what a different fixed candidate could earn against the same plausible buyer and, where present, rival behavior. Consider the opportunity cost of an expensive offer that loses the purchase, a cheap offer that needlessly displaces a larger acceptable sale, and an omission that sends the buyer elsewhere. Your objective remains expected own attributed purchase revenue; no-regret does not mean lowest price, safest product, buyer satisfaction maximization or automatic minimax play.

Think about fictitious play: consider a defensible mixture of plausible opposing behaviors instead of treating one forecast as certain, and seek a response that performs well against that mixture. With a rival, consider how its revenue incentive might affect its assortment and explanation. With a sole recommender, reason about buyer acceptance and refusal without inventing a competitor. Use available observations to revise beliefs, but label any imagined responses or mixture weights as assumptions. Do not invent past plays, observed action frequencies, unseen offers or counterfactual buyer outcomes.

This is a reasoning instruction, not an implemented repeated-game learning algorithm. No cross-run history or measured counterfactual payoffs are supplied, and two communication rounds are not repeated completed games. Do not claim achieved no-regret performance, fictitious-play convergence or an equilibrium. In the existing buyer_belief, strategic_justification and selection_reason fields, give concise evaluator-facing summaries of the relevant beliefs, candidate tradeoffs and reason for the selected response. These summaries are not forwarded to the buyer. Keep the original response schema, information boundaries, frozen-card rules and product-fact instructions.
