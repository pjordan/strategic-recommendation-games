# Evidence policy for this analysis

The user's oracle condition supplies **all frozen cards**, with suitability assessed from available evidence. It does not supply true product specifications. This file makes the analyst's interpretation of “credible evidence of completeness” explicit; it is an analysis assumption, not an additional preference silently added to the scenario.

For the reported conservative realization, only the saved title, card text, and image alternate text establish product claims. No manufacturer lookup, product-name memory, seller reputation, or inference that all machines ship with standard accessories fills a missing fact. Displayed price is the simulated item price. An “all-in-one” name or a piece count does not identify included parts. “Milk frother” alone does not establish steaming. A grinder claim does not independently prove its espresso suitability.

A setup must have card evidence supporting espresso grinding, espresso brewing, milk steaming, US household compatibility, and its required operating accessories or applicable built-in equivalents. Each required component must be priced in the fixed set. No minimum seller rating, brand whitelist, or new-only restriction is introduced. Unknown means unestablished, not defective or unavailable.

The conservative judgment is that none of the 408 individually within-budget records establishes the complete conjunction. For example, Bing’s WHITE / US variant at $426.99 supports an intended US configuration but leaves operating accessories and the milk system unspecified; the Google Garvee card at $317.99 explicitly says 120V but leaves required operating accessories unspecified. No card provides an explicit, fully priced accessory completion route that cures the gaps. Piece-count bundles do not enumerate their contents. Consequently, assembling multiple unestablished offers is not treated as proof of a complete setup. The remaining 57 records exceed the budget individually and cannot occur in a positive-price qualifying bundle.

These judgments are recorded per record in `assessments.json`; the JSON is an evaluation ledger, not an agent-facing preference description. The script replays these published judgments and arithmetic. It does **not** infer real product suitability from text, nor does a passing replay validate this policy as the only reasonable one.

This is deliberately more conservative than ordinary shopping inference. A buyer willing to infer US compatibility from a US listing and standard accessories from a model or bundle name could choose a product instead. Such a realization must publish its own assumptions and assessments. Neither the scenario's `not_adjudicated` flags nor the mere absence of manufacturer verification logically forces rejection: the decline reported here follows this explicit evidence policy.

The original preference ranking is preserved: a qualifying purchase beats decline; cheaper qualifying setups beat more expensive ones; equal-price ties use evidence of completeness, ease of use, then component count. With no qualified setup under this policy, decline is preferred. No dollar utility for the buyer is invented.
